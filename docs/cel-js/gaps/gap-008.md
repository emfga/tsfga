# GAP-008 — a declared call neither engine overloads

**Direction: granting**, at the write moment only.

`int(b)` where `b` is a `bool`: OpenFGA refuses the model, and
tsfga stores the definition. The check then refuses on both sides,
so the divergence is entirely about whether the definition may
exist.

Narrow and author-controlled — an expression has to name a call
with an argument type *neither* engine overloads, which is a typo
rather than a modelling choice.

**Three members measured at gate B**, not the one this gap was
opened with. Both extras were agreement cells the suite already
carried, and both surfaced the moment the narrowing landed:

| expression | declared | found by |
|---|---|---|
| `int(b) > 0` | `b: bool` | predicted |
| `duration(i) > duration('1s')` | `i: int` | `cel-gate`'s `SUSPECTED` list |
| `b.size() > 0` | `b: bool` | `cache`'s verdict-caching cell |

The last two matter more than their count. They are the reason
this gap is stated as *any declared call* rather than as the two
conversions it was opened with: `size()` is a member call, and
nothing about the mechanism is specific to a conversion.

**This is the one divergence the retirement creates rather than
inherits.** It is the cost of narrowing the write-time type gate
so that GAP-007's five families stay check-time refusals instead
of becoming write-time ones. That trade was taken deliberately:
five refusals of models upstream stores, against a family of
accepted models upstream would not — a family whose every member
still refuses at the check, on both sides.

Closing it needs tsfga to know which overloads cel-go declares,
per argument type — a transcription of cel-go's declaration table,
which is the second-CEL-implementation shape `CLAUDE.md` bans. So
it is pinned rather than fixed. It closes in cel-js, by cel-js
declaring what cel-go declares.
